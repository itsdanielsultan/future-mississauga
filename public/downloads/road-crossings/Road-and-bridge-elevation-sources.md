# Road and bridge elevation sources

Reviewed September 21, 2026. This note supports the Road Crossings revision. Source downloads and screening are separate from a completed geometry repair.

## Why the dips occur

The inherited context builder places every `RoadPaved` vertex at the bare-earth DTM plus a 0.3 m display offset. That is suitable for ground-supported roads, but a bridge needs its own elevated deck. The inherited supplementary bridge layer contains only 17 accepted spans. Its extractor requires paired straight edges, at least 75% road overlap, a median DSM–DTM separation between 1.5 m and 18 m, and a 90th-percentile plane residual of at most 1.2 m. Curved or fragmented boundaries and noisy low-resolution edge samples can fail those checks. A failure to create a supplemental deck leaves the original terrain-draped road in place.

Ontario describes the DTM as bare-earth terrain with constructed features removed. The DSM is the first reflective surface and can contain elevated infrastructure, vehicles, trees and roofs. Consequently, raising all roads to the DSM would create different errors. Source: [Ontario flooding survey and mapping technical bulletin, December 2023](https://ero.ontario.ca/public/2023-11/Final_Technical-Bulletin-Flood-Hazard-Data-Surveying-and-Mapping-Guidance_v1.0_December_2023_FINAL-s.pdf), [Ontario DSM catalogue](https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/ontario-digital-surface-model-lidar-derived).

## Bounded native-resolution preparation

The research downloader creates 0.5 m Float32 TIFF patches around the 64 screened street candidates, merging nearby windows and splitting any extent wider than 1,600 m. It adds wider Burnhamthorpe and Highway 403 Credit River windows. It does not download a full-city 0.5 m mosaic. The first 96 rasters across 48 windows cover all 64 original candidates (432,047,664 bytes). A finite, citywide source-coverage screen then identified 46 additional groups outside those windows. Another 64 rasters across 32 merged windows cover those groups (235,378,096 bytes). The original preparation set contains 160 rasters across 80 windows, totalling 667,425,760 bytes. A final paired patch of 1,575,404 bytes completes the full extent of an already-listed 409 component beyond its earlier peak-centred window. The completed set is 162 rasters across 81 windows, totalling 669,001,164 bytes; the finite screening inventory is unchanged. All downloaded file hashes and exact 0.5 m pixel spacing passed validation. This completes source preparation; it does not imply that every candidate needs a raised deck. The patch manifest records every request, exact raster extent, width, height, bytes, hash, primary source raster and errors. A subsequent check verified every file hash, 0.5 m output pixel spacing and primary-only source selection. Other candidate locations include 2018 and 2023 primary data; inspect each patch rather than applying the Credit River dates citywide. No source elevations are re-labelled as a survey undertaken in 2026.

Each export locks to the intersecting `Category=1` primary rasters, uses the `None` rendering function and nearest-neighbour sampling. This avoids requesting a coloured relief image or an overview rather than real elevation values. At the checked Burnhamthorpe, Highway 403 and QEW Credit River locations, the primary DSM tile is GTA 2015, 0.5 m. Records labelled 2023 in those queries are `Category=2` overviews; they do not establish a 2023 local acquisition. The Burnhamthorpe point-cloud tile confirms acquisition on April 25, 2015.

Files: `work/road-crossings/research/native-patches-manifest.json`, `native-extra-patches-manifest.json`, their window/QA files, and the `native-patches/` and `native-patches-extra/` folders. The raw downloads are preparation data, not public as-built bridge geometry.

## Finite coverage inventory

The independent screen inspected 12,836 City paved-road polygons, 1,158 river/shoreline polygons, 8,391 bridge-edge features and 19,880 street segments. There were no literal paved-road/water polygon intersections larger than 2 m²: the source water mapping is cut around road crossings. To catch those gaps, every bridge edge was sampled at intervals no greater than 5 m, with offsets of 0, ±4, ±10 and ±18 m; points were retained only inside mapped pavement and within 80 m of mapped water. Coarse DSM–DTM differences greater than 1 m produced 111 spatial/name groups. All 111 screening peaks now have native elevation coverage. The downloadable [source coverage inventory](Source-coverage-inventory.json) preserves the group bounds, street and bridge FIDs, and patch references.

This is a completeness screen of mapped bridge/pavement near water, not a certified bridge register and not an instruction to lift 111 surfaces. It includes road medians, vegetation-contaminated pixels, stacked roads, causeways and culverts. A nearby pond does not establish that a road spans that pond. Ordinary gradients without bridge/water evidence are outside this finite review. Correction decisions and remaining unresolved cases are recorded separately by the implementation audit.

- **Burnhamthorpe Road West over the Credit River**, around 43.5605, -79.6730: the City confirms twin bridges. Source bridge-edge FIDs include 9506, 9507, 9642, 8862 and 8851. The old 5 m raster produces substantial and variable DSM–DTM separation on their edges; native interior samples are needed before assigning deck levels. [City construction notice, 2023–2024](https://www6.mississauga.ca/Public_Notices/2023/Construction%20Notice-Burnhamthorpe%20Bridges%20over%20Credit%20River%20-%20Stage%201.pdf).
- **Burnhamthorpe Road West over Mullet Creek**, near source EPSG:26917 607017, 4823229, and the nearby Sawmill Valley crossing: treat the separate structures individually. The City retrofit documentation identifies both Credit River and Mullet Creek bridges. [City retrofit project](https://www.mississauga.ca/file/COM/Retrofit-Burnhamthorpe-Road-Bridges.pdf).
- **Highway 403 over the Credit River**, around 43.5627, -79.6835: include both carriageways and their continuous approaches, not only one short accepted edge pair. Existing source lines include 9601, 9532 and 9676. The native source patches cover the wider crossing.
- **QEW over the Credit River**, around 43.5566, -79.6103: 2015 surface data predates the new twin. Infrastructure Ontario records substantial completion on June 26, 2025, but MTO's 2026 project update distinguishes the new twin carrying both directions from remaining work and unopened lanes on the historic bridge. The north–south pedestrian bridge also remains part of the unfinished work. Do not use the old DSM alone to reconstruct the completed new arrangement. [Infrastructure Ontario project](https://www.infrastructureontario.ca/en/what-we-do/projectssearch/qewcredit-river-improvement-project/), [MTO project update](https://qewcreditriver.ca/news/), [dated project photographs](https://qewcreditriver.ca/project-gallery/).
- **Lakeshore Road, Dundas Street, Eglinton Avenue and Queensway river crossings** require the same distinction between roadway, banks, water, and structures above or below. Named engineering records can validate individual deck/soffit levels but must match the actual crossing and the actual built option. The City's Dixie–Dundas structural assessment is an example of usable engineering sections, not a citywide height dataset. [Dixie–Dundas structural assessment](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/25150712/dixie-dundas-structural-assessments.pdf).

## QEW new and historic bridge registration

[MTO's March 2019 foundation investigation and design report, GeoCRES 30M12-426](https://foundation.mto.gov.on.ca/Data?path=foundations%2F30M12%2F30M12-426%2F&name=1662333%20FIDR%20Credit%20River%20Bridge%202019March01.pdf&handler=GetDocument) describes the proposed twin immediately north of the historic bridge, separated by about 2 m. It specifies 68 m end spans and a 118 m centre span. PDF pages 31 and 48 give a proposed west-to-east grade of approximately 96.1 to 94.8 m, based on the 2018 general arrangement. These are historical design values, not verified current deck survey heights; the report identifies only a Geodetic vertical datum. They must not be silently substituted for CGVD2013 elevations.

The report's plan and borehole table on PDF page 64 were registered from NAD83 MTM Zone 10 to UTM Zone 17 and checked visually against City 2024 bridge edges. This resolves the four parallel edge chains: the northern pair belongs to the new twin and the southern pair to the historic bridge. New twin south edge: FIDs 7677 and 7678. New twin north edge: FIDs 7770, 7777, 7778, 7856, 7857, 7858, 7859 and 7867. Historic north edge and south edge consist of further fragmented chains recorded in [the registration evidence](QEW-bridge-registration.json). The method preserves original coordinates and does not change any model geometry. Where the new footprint lies outside the old scan's elevated surface, verified approach interpolation remains an explicitly disclosed representation, with the engineering profile as a reasonableness check.

## Water beneath corrected decks

The [Ontario Hydro Network Waterbody layer](https://ws.lioservices.lrc.gov.on.ca/arcgis2/rest/services/LIO_OPEN_DATA/LIO_Open01/MapServer/25) independently maps Credit River as a permanent river. The retrieved feature, OGF_ID 1201496694, records horizontal accuracy within 5 m and a geometry-update timestamp of June 22, 2026. That timestamp is an edit date, not a new elevation or water-level survey. This 2D polygon can support a narrowly clipped river underlay where City mapping stops at a verified bridge gap. It does not justify changing the full shoreline, raising the river to the bridge, or assigning a measured water elevation. The implementation review determines the actual clipped areas used; the research retrieval itself changed no geometry.

The [independent non-fit source review](Nonfit-source-review.json) classified all 24 failed automatic-extraction rows. Four contain actionable upper-road families for a corrected extractor; two more lie outside City land with incomplete City footprint coverage. The others include already-linked lower roads, railway returns, nearby structures, three sampled cases without a supported 1 m model-grade deficit, and two small coarse-terrain grade dips at Atwater Avenue and Derry Road West. Those categories prevent lifting the wrong road. Their index numbers refer to the 112-row native extraction input, not the 111-group spatial coverage inventory.


A separate [two-profile follow-up](Residual-source-review.json) resolves two difficult source patterns. At Wiseman Court, 19 of 32 native paved samples define a near-flat surface around 103.74 m ASL beneath canopy returns; this is up to 3.47 m above the coarse model DTM at sampled points. At the widened Highway 401 lane, City 2024 pavement extends over canopy/ground in the 2015 scan. [Infrastructure Ontario](https://www.infrastructureontario.ca/en/what-we-do/projectssearch/highway-401-expansion-project/) dates construction from November 2019 to October 2023. That lane therefore needs a disclosed source-constrained approximation or newer survey evidence, not a fit to old trees. Exact final application and continuity remain implementation checks.


[Final grade evidence](Final-grade-source-evidence.json) records the source-supported local corrections and their limits. Atwater Avenue and Derry Road West have small paved-grade patches with native-plane 90th-percentile residuals of about 0.06 m and 0.05 m. For the post-scan Highway 401 footprint, an intact adjacent-carriageway plane uses 6,929 native samples; its 90th-percentile residual is 0.09 m on the old pavement. The estimate transfers at most 35.32 m to the exact 585.57 m² new-lane footprint. That residual is not an accuracy claim for the new lane's height or crossfall. The final 409 component is ground-supported with a curved vertical profile; discrete elevated first returns consistent with vehicles do not justify raising it. All final changed road surfaces still require the implementation's approach, crossing-order and topology checks.

## Recommended correction and verification

Use City bridge boundaries and the road network to identify a complete span and its approaches. Sample the native DSM inside the paved bridge, away from edge parapets and nearby trees. Verify a coherent deck against approach levels and known crossing order. Use a robust longitudinal/planar fit where justified; retain curves and divided carriageways. Replace only the erroneous bridge road surface, while keeping the river, valley and any lower road below it. Do not resolve the symptom by raising water, filling valleys, or clipping all low geometry.

Any approach interpolation or deck thickness without engineering measurements remains an explicitly labelled display approximation. A straight line between reliable approach elevations can be a fallback envelope only after the mapped bridge extent and crossing order are confirmed. It is not a surveyed structure. Source-supported flat decks should not be rejected simply because the ravine is more than 18 m below them.

For each corrected crossing, record source FIDs and dates; profile road heights before/after; test deck-to-approach continuity, the bridge surface's clearance above water and lower routes, unchanged horizontal footprints and normal winding; inspect oblique and underside views. Also remove or mask the old dipped road within the corrected footprint so two road surfaces are not left stacked in the scene.
