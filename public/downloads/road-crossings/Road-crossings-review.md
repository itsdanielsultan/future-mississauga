# Road and water crossings · 21 September 2026

The earlier road surface followed the bare-earth terrain beneath bridges. This revision corrects those valley dips using mapped road and bridge outlines, native elevation evidence, and bounded approach transitions. Roads underneath an overpass retain their separate lower level. Existing terrain and water meshes are preserved.

The working register contains **107 profile or strip records**, not 107 distinct bridges. Divided carriageways and adjoining pieces can describe one crossing. The finite coverage review examines 111 spatial/name groups from 12,836 mapped paved-road polygons, 1,158 water polygons and 8,391 mapped bridge-edge features. Water polygons are often cut around roads, so a literal road–water intersection test alone misses real crossings. See the [coverage decisions](Road-crossing-coverage.json) and [individual profiles](Road-crossing-profiles.json).

Native evidence comprises 81 bounded windows and 162 DSM/DTM rasters at 0.5 m spacing. Primary acquisitions include 2015, 2018 and 2023; a newer overview date is not treated as a newer local survey. The [source note](Road-and-bridge-elevation-sources.md) explains the public City and Ontario sources. These are historical measured surfaces, not a 2026 engineering or as-built survey. Trees and vehicles were excluded where their returns conflicted with coherent pavement. One Highway 409 component was deliberately left on its existing curved ground profile after a complete patch showed that its apparent high spots were vehicles.

Six records use explicitly estimated newer-road levels: the operating QEW north twin and five widened Highway 401 strips whose older elevation survey predates the newer mapped pavement. The QEW profile interpolates same-datum approach levels; its registered MTO design is corroborating evidence, not an as-built elevation. The Highway 401 estimates transfer the measured grade of adjacent original carriageways. Their small fit residuals describe that supporting pavement, not the accuracy of the newer lanes. Current crown, crossfall and structural dimensions remain unverified. The historic QEW south bridge is represented separately as a closed structure, supported by its mapped edges and older native surface.

Two small Atwater and Derry corrections address coarsening of the ground-level road surface, without claiming an aerial bridge. Two incomplete structures outside the City boundary remain external-context limitations. Railway structures and nearby roads at another level are not indiscriminately raised.

Local road triangulation is refined where needed to preserve a short span’s profile. Source horizontal outlines stay fixed. Grade-separated roads use a distinct upper surface while retaining mapped lower roadway. Lower-road protection is shared across adjoining deck pieces so that one piece cannot cut another’s protected road. A measured component’s core takes priority over a neighbouring strip’s estimated approach; triangles follow those boundaries instead of interpolating between unrelated levels. Approach heights blend back to the original road. No imaginary piers, surveyed deck thicknesses or new crossings are added.

Two broader Highway 409 profile records overlapped smaller profiles of the same curved pavement. The shared City road/bridge identifiers and native returns support the smaller local fits. Their exact overlap areas now have one documented owner, with a 45 m transition from the adjacent broader fit. Original footprints and plane coefficients remain in the register as provenance. These are duplicate-fit corrections, not additional bridges or evidence of two road levels. Coverage is checked against the original footprints as well as the effective owner footprints.

A bounded bare-earth underlay fills earlier terrain cutouts beneath raised paved footprints. Existing terrain vertices remain unchanged. Under selected Credit River bridge gaps, a separate water underlay is clipped to the official Ontario Hydro Network river footprint and linked to adjacent existing water display levels. New bare earth is excluded from that river-gap footprint. The water elevation is estimated; the Ontario river footprint itself reports accuracy within 5 m. This is a limited continuity repair, not a river or hydrology redesign. In the close-up views, the shaded lower band is restored terrain, not a duplicate sagging road. The two Burnhamthorpe carriageways preserve the City’s separate approximately 7.4–7.9 m paved widths; their unmodelled structural details are not replaced with an invented broad deck.

Mapped road paint and associated parallel sidewalks follow their repaired support surfaces with small display offsets. Seven superseded older bridge-overlay surfaces are removed to avoid duplicate decks; ten unrelated source overlays remain. The Transitway’s road-running sections receive matching highway corrections, while dedicated busway sections can match only a mapped Transitway profile, not a nearby parallel highway. GO and HML geometry remains byte-identical to the prior edition. Horizontal transit alignment, system colours and depth behavior are preserved. See the [dependent-layer checks](Dependent-layer-QA.json) and [context summary](Context-road-correction-summary.json).

The profile field named `illustrativeHeightAllowanceM` is a modelling allowance, not a statistical confidence interval or surveyed error bound. Raw residuals may include rejected canopy or vehicle returns; inlier residuals are identified separately. The model remains a source-based city visualization with disclosed approximations, rather than a certified engineering model.

The final checks passed [independent source and geometry sampling](Independent-context-QA.json), [original-footprint coverage and ownership](Original-footprint-coverage-QA.json), and byte comparisons between the 15 locally packaged detail models and their current interactive sources. All 6,155 sampled core points are covered, and all 1,208 sampled protected lower-road points retain coverage. A separate [composite continuity check](Composite-field-continuity-QA.json) tested 29,419 half-metre samples inside the two reviewed transition footprints; their heights stay within the documented native-plane envelope. These checks verify the disclosed reconstruction and its coverage; they do not turn the historical data or estimated profiles into a current engineering survey.
